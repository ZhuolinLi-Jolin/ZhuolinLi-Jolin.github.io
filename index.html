Final Project

An in-depth analytical exploration

Are AI Taking Our Jobs?

This project investigates the widespread fear of automation through a data-driven lens, analyzing the relationship between AI adoption and occupational stability. Unlike previous industrial shifts, Generative AI targets different roles. Through a series of interactive visualizations integrating O*NET task data with BLS employment statistics, we explore whether we are witnessing a simple replacement of labor, or a complex structural reorganization of the workforce.

Research Question

How is AI driving a structural shift in the US labor market?

FIGURE 1

AI Exposure vs. Wage: A Structural Shift

Interactive visualization analyzing the correlation between AI Exposure Scores (AIOE) and Annual Mean Wages across different job sectors (Logarithmic Scale).

FIGURE 2

Relationship Between AI Exposure and Occupational Mix Change

Scattered distribution analysis of AI Exposure Index and Occupational Mix Change Index of major industries in the United States.

FIGURE 3

US Unemployment Composition by Industry and AI Exposure Score (2023–2025)

This stacked area chart illustrates the dynamics of the US labor market. The top boundary of the chart tracks the fluctuations in total US unemployment from December 2022 (following the release of ChatGPT) through 2025.Each color band represents a specific industry, where the vertical thickness of the band indicates the volume of unemployment within that sector at any given time. The color gradient corresponds directly to each industry's AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) score. This distribution allows for a direct observation of whether the unemployed population is concentrated in sectors highly susceptible to AI (High AIOE) or in industries less affected, such as manual labor (Low AIOE).

FIGURE 4

Distribution of Major Occupational Categories Across AI Exposure and Usage Quadrants

This diagram illustrates the mapping of occupational categories into the four-quadrant framework of AI exposure and utilization, highlighting how different labor sectors are structurally positioned relative to AI's technological impact and adoption.

FIGURE 5

AI Adoption Gap by Occupational Category

Arrows illustrate the deviation of actual AI usage from technical expectations, identifying sectors that are either leading in adoption (green) or under-utilizing their potential (red).

FIGURE 6

Structural Intensity Heatmap: AI Exposure Shifts by Unemployment Duration

This visualization provides empirical evidence that the impact of AI on the labor market is both lagged and cumulative. The concentration of automation risk among the long-term unemployed suggests that AI is not merely causing frictional churn but is fundamentally altering the unemployment structure. This slow but persistent re-composition of the labor force captures the very essence of a "Structural Shift" in the modern economy.

Key Findings

The analysis reveals that AI adoption is driving a structural transformation in the labor market. Jobs with high AI exposure are not only facing wage stagnation but also significant shifts in occupational composition……

FIGURE CC5

Scraper and APIs

Implement one chart using live data from an API and another using data scraped from a website via Python (Google Colab).

API Functional Description

The chart retrieves employment data via the World Bank Indicators API, using a RESTful structure based on the URL https://api.worldbank.org/v2/.

To execute a data call, specific path segments are appended to define the scope: /country/ followed by ISO-3 codes (e.g., usa;ind for the U.S. and India) and /indicator/ followed by the metric ID (SL.EMP.TOTL.SP.ZS for employment to population ratio). The request is further refined with query parameters: format=json for machine-readable data, date=2000:2025 to set the temporal range, and per_page=160 to capture all data points in one response.

Full Final URL:

https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/usa;ind/indicator/SL.EMP.TOTL.SP.ZS?format=json&date=2000:2025&per_page=160

Scraped Wales GVA data. Navigating complex HTML tags to pinpoint target data was challenging, requiring precise cell isolation before converting into a tidy long-format CSV.
